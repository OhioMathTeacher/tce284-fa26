Longing, Three Drafts

Let the thread talk · Todd Edwards

My thread is searching for my biological mother. Here is the same thread, three times. The first is the gush. The second lets the thread say "I." The third is what happened after the answer came — because the longing didn't stop. Drafts 2 and 3 were worked out in conversation with Claude.

Draft 1

The gush. Longing is an it in the corner — I'm describing it from outside.

Longing is standing in the corner.It's thumbing its nose at you as you try to get your work done.It asks, "Who are you?"Knowing full well that you don't know yourself."Who will I become?""Where am I from?"If only I knew who I was, then maybe I could become the man I was supposed to be.

Draft 2

Longing speaks. It names itself in the first line, so every "I" is Longing's. "The man I was supposed to be" moves into its mouth.

Longing

I'm Longing. I stand in the corner of every room you work in.I don't help. I thumb my nose.You type, and I ask, Who are you?I already know you don't know.

In the evenings I ask it softer: Where are you from?I ask it at the doctor's office,on the line that says family history,where you write unknownand I lean over your shoulder and read it twice.

I carry a face aroundand hold it up next to yours.I don't know whose it is.Neither do you.

Who will you become? I save that one for last,because it's the cruelest.You think the answer is on the other side of me —that if you knew who you were,you'd finally become the man you were supposed to be.

I let you think that.I'm not going anywhere.

Draft 3

The truth about the ending: the answer came, and the longing stayed. It just changed what it asks for.

Longing

I'm Longing. I stand in the corner of every room you work in.I don't help. I thumb my nose.For years I asked, Who are you?I knew you didn't know.

I asked it softer in the evenings: Where are you from?I asked it on every format the line that says family history,where you wrote unknownand I leaned over your shoulder and read it twice.

Who will you become? I saved that one for last.You thought the answer was on the other side of me —that if you knew who you were,you'd finally become the man you were supposed to be.

I let you think that.

Then you found out.A name. Finally, a face.You thought I'd pack up and go.

I didn't.I just changed what I ask for.Now I stand in the corner of a kitchen you never grew up in,at a birthday party nobody got to throw,and I ask for the one thingno answer can give back.

I'm not going anywhere.
