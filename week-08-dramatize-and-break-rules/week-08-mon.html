Week 8 · Monday — Render the Scenes That Carry the Weight

Act II · currere · show, don't tell — in your voice
Today's goals: Take the six moments you roughed over break and render the one or two the whole thread leans on — make us see and hear them, in your voice.

As you come in: count off 1 to 7. Your number is your group of three for today's two check-ins.

0 · Daily Poem — Write the wrong poem (~10 min)

  • One card per pair. Each card holds a few words from a poem you haven't read. In 90 seconds, write the one line that comes right before your card.
  • The class poem. Pair by pair, read your line and then your card. No talking in between — just listen to the poem the room wrote.
  • The real one. Then hear the poem those words came from.
  • With your partner: Same words. What is the poet doing with them that you weren't?
  • Steal the move. Take one of your six scenes and write one line that does what the poet did. A few read aloud.

Purpose: seek surprise — get surprised by a poem as a reader, then borrow how it surprised you for one of your own scenes.

1 · Set up your currere doc — and find the weight (~10 min)

  • Start one Google Doc for your currere. This is where you write today, and it becomes your First Draft on Friday.
  • Photograph your six cards and the chart-paper sheet, and put each card's photo in the doc, one after another. (On your phone, the Google Docs app does it: ＋ → Image → Camera.)
  • Under each photo, paste the rough scene you drafted for it. No draft for a card yet? Write one sentence that tells what happened in it. That sentence is what you'll render in section 2.
  • Read across the six. Mark the one or two scenes your currere can't do without — the ones where, if you cut them, the rest would stop making sense to a reader. Not every scene needs the same polish; those one or two are where today's craft goes.

Purpose: spend your craft where it counts — render the moments the thread depends on, don't over-polish the transitions.

2 · Show, don't tell — render it (~30 min)

  • Write (10 min). Take a load-bearing moment. Find the place where you told us — where you named a feeling ("I was nervous," "it mattered to me") or summarized what happened. Now show it instead: the sensory detail, the action, a line of what was actually said. Put us in the room. (This is the Show, Don't Tell move from Act I — you've done it before.)
  • Check-in: before and after (10 min). In your group of three, each person reads the told sentence, then the rendered one. Listeners say two things only: "I could see ___." "I still need to see ___."
  • Write (10 min). Go after what your group still needed to see.

Purpose: turn a summarized moment into a dramatized one — the difference between a reader being told the thread and a reader feeling it.

3 · Voice-print pass (~30 min)

  • Write (10 min). Read your rendered scene aloud. Where does it sound like anyone — like a report, like the flattened version? Mark those lines. Bend one back toward your voice — the rhythm and word-choice that are yours (your Voice Print from Act I).
  • Check-in: which one's you? (10 min). Back in your group of three, read one line twice — the flat version and your version, in either order. The group guesses which one is really you. If they can't tell, the bend isn't done yet.
  • Write (10 min). Carry that voice through the rest of the scene. The moment should sound like you telling it, not like a summary of you.

Purpose: a rendered scene in a borrowed voice still isn't yours — read it aloud and put your voice back in.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
  • Render your other load-bearing moment the same way. Keep the connective scenes light.
  • Bring your six cards to class Wednesday — or the photos in your doc. You'll be working from one of them directly.
