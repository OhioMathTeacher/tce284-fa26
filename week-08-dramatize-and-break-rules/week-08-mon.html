Act II · currere · show, don't tell — in your voice
Today's goals: Take the six moments you roughed over break and render the one or two the whole thread leans on — make us see and hear them, in your voice.

0 · Daily Poem — Mystery Poem (~20 min) · slides (private link)
  • With a neighbor: examine the words highlighted in the mystery poem. What do the words convey? What might this poem be about? Why do you think that?
  • Read the whole poem.
  • Same words. With your partner: what is the poet doing with them that you weren't?
  • The move.
  • Steal the move (5 min). Gush it into a moment from one of your six scenes.
  • Share (5 min). A few read aloud.

Purpose: seek surprise — get surprised by a poem as a reader, then borrow how it surprised you for one of your own scenes.

1 · Set up your currere doc — and find the weight (~10 min)
  • Start one Google Doc for your currere. This is where you write today, and it becomes your First Draft.
  • Photograph your six cards and the chart-paper sheet. At minimum, your doc has six pages, one card photo on each. (On your phone, the Google Docs app does it: ＋ → Image → Camera.)
  • Under each photo, paste the rough scene you drafted for it. No draft for a card yet? Write one sentence that tells what happened in it. That sentence is what you'll render in section 2.
  • Read across the six. Mark the one or two scenes your currere can't do without — the ones where, if you cut them, the rest would stop making sense to a reader. Not every scene needs the same polish; those one or two are where today's craft goes.

Purpose: spend your craft where it counts — render the moments the thread depends on, don't over-polish the transitions.

2 · Show, don't tell — render it (~20 min)
  • Write (10 min), in your Google Doc. Take one of the scenes you marked in section 1. Find the place where you told us — where you named a feeling ("I was nervous," "it mattered to me") or summarized what happened. Now show it instead: the sensory detail, the action, a line of what was actually said. Put us in the room. (This is the Show, Don't Tell move from Act I — you've done it before.)
  • Check-in: before and after (10 min). In your group of three, each person reads the told sentence, then the rendered one. Listeners say two things only: "I could see ___." "I still need to see ___."

Purpose: turn a summarized moment into a dramatized one — the difference between a reader being told the thread and a reader feeling it.

3 · Voice-print pass (~25 min)
  • Write (10 min), in your Google Doc. Same scene, second pass. Read the scene you just rendered aloud. Where does it sound like anyone — like a report, like the flattened version? Mark those lines. Bend one back toward your voice — the rhythm and word-choice that are yours (your Voice Print from Act I).
  • Check-in: which one's you? (10 min). Back in your group of three, read one line twice — the flat version and your version, in either order. The group guesses which one is really you. If they can't tell, the bend isn't done yet.
  • Write (5 min), in your Google Doc. Carry that voice through the rest of the scene. The moment should sound like you telling it, not like a summary of you.

Purpose: a rendered scene in a borrowed voice still isn't yours — read it aloud and put your voice back in.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
  • Render your other load-bearing moment the same way. Keep the connective scenes light.
  • First Draft due Tue Oct 20, 11:59 p.m. — bring a complete draft to class next Monday for peer review first. Where We're Going

  • Why keep the paper? The paper is your gush. A pen can't delete, so you keep moving instead of fixing — the same reason Journaler locks editing in gush mode. And on paper you can spread all six out and see the whole thread at once. The Doc is the next step: where you pick the best bits and shape them. Gush on paper; build in the Doc.
