Week 8 · Monday — Render the Scenes That Carry the Weight

Act II · currere · show, don't tell — in your voice
Today's goals: Take the six moments you roughed over break and render the one or two the whole thread leans on — make us see and hear them, in your voice.

0 · Daily Poem — "The Gift" (~10 min)

  • Li-Young Lee, "The Gift". Read it aloud. One remembered moment — a father pulling a splinter — carries a whole life of tenderness. Where does the poem show instead of tell? Notice how it moves between past and present without ever announcing the years.

Purpose: a single moment, fully rendered, doing all the work — exactly what a load-bearing scene in your currere is asked to do.

1 · Which scenes carry the weight? (~10 min)

  • Lay out your six rough scenes from over break. Read across them. Mark the one or two the thread leans on hardest — the moments a reader has to feel for the rest to make sense.
  • Not every scene needs the same polish. Some are quick connective moments; one or two are load-bearing. Those are where today's craft goes.

Purpose: spend your craft where it counts — render the moments the thread depends on, don't over-polish the transitions.

2 · Show, don't tell — render it (~30 min)

  • Take a load-bearing moment. Find the place where you told us — where you named a feeling ("I was nervous," "it mattered to me") or summarized what happened.
  • Now show it instead: the sensory detail, the action, a line of what was actually said. Put us in the room. (This is the Show, Don't Tell move from Act I — you've done it before.)
  • Trade one rendered line with a partner. Ask: can you see it? What do you still need to see?

Purpose: turn a summarized moment into a dramatized one — the difference between a reader being told the thread and a reader feeling it.

3 · Voice-print pass (~20 min)

  • Read your rendered scene aloud. Where does it sound like anyone — like a report, like the flattened version? Mark those lines.
  • Bend them back toward your voice — the rhythm and word-choice that are yours (your Voice Print from Act I). The moment should sound like you telling it, not like a summary of you.

Purpose: a rendered scene in a borrowed voice still isn't yours — read it aloud and put your voice back in.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
  • Render your other load-bearing moment the same way. Keep the connective scenes light.
  • Photograph your six cards and your chart-paper sheet — you'll want the drawings and the write-around, and paper gets lost.
  • Bring your six cards to class Wednesday. You'll be working from one of them directly.
