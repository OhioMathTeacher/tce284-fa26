Week 8 · Wednesday — Break a Rule, Share the Room

Act II · currere · Grammar B, on purpose
Have your six cards with you today. You'll pull one and write straight from it.
Today's goals: Remember the four Grammar B moves, freewrite one of your six moments while breaking a rule on purpose, share a little aloud, and name what's gotten clearer about your writing.

0 · Daily Poem — "[since feeling is first]" (~10 min)

  • E. E. Cummings, "[since feeling is first]". Read it aloud. Watch what he does to grammar on purpose — no capitals, the syntax bent to the feeling: "who pays any attention / to the syntax of things / will never wholly kiss you." When does breaking the rule tell it truer than the rule would?

Purpose: the syntax-breaker's own argument for breaking syntax — exactly the permission today's freewrite runs on.

1 · Grammar B — you already own these (~10 min)

  • Quick refresher, not a re-teach. The four moves from Breaking the Rules: sentence fragments · labyrinthine sentence · purposeful misspelling · double voice. Glance back at the Grammar B examples if you need a jog.
  • Ask yourself: which of these might a moment in my currere want? Where would a broken rule tell it truer?

Purpose: bring the tools you already felt in Act I back into the room, aimed now at the currere.

2 · Freewrite — one card, one broken rule (~20 min)

  • Take one of your six moments — one card, in front of you. In Journaler, start the timer and freewrite it fully, breaking at least one rule on purpose.
  • Gush mode locks editing, so break freely — let a fragment land, let one sentence run on and on, bend a word so its spelling carries the meaning, run two voices at once. Every "error" is one you mean.

Purpose: find out what a rule-broken version of the moment can do that a clean one can't — before you decide whether it stays.

3 · Share it out (~15 min)

  • A few volunteers read the rule-broken moment aloud. The room names which move it used — and whether the broken rule earns its keep, or just gets in the way.

Purpose: hear the moves out loud in each other's work — a broken rule reads differently than it writes, and the room can tell you if it lands.

4 · Around the room (~25 min)

  • Each person offers one thing: a line of your own writing you're proud of, or an idea about writing that's gotten clearer across the currere so far.
  • No commentary needed — just put it in the room. We've been heads-down in six moments each; today we hear the whole class at once.

Purpose: the last whole-group moment before you go solo on the draft — name what the currere has taught you before you assemble it.

Wrap-up & before Friday:
  • Assemble your six moments into your First Draft — a whole strand, not six chunks in a row. Let the thread carry the reader across the moments.
  • Bring a Todd-in-a-Can review of your draft to class. First Draft due Fri · Canvas.
  • Read WWM ch16, "Metaphor" · ch17, "Leading the Way." Monday we read leads and metaphor aloud — the two lenses you revise the draft with.
