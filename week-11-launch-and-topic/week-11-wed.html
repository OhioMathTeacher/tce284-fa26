Week 11 · Wednesday — Topic, Map & Sources

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · the Topic Map · choose your topic · the three rings · JSTOR · Gemini Notebook
Today's goals: Choose your topic, work through its Topic Map, start looking for sources, and set up your Gemini Notebook.

1 · The Topic Map, together (~15 min)
  • I'll share my Topic Map on the doc cam. Then a few of you will share yours.
  • Together: what filled in easily? Where did you get stuck?

Purpose: see what an unfinished Topic Map looks like before you go back to yours.

2 · Topic Map Studio: the front side (~15 min)
  • Revise the map you started after Monday's class.
  • Fill in the front side for 1 or 2 more of your topics.
  • Front side only for now. Go for quantity. You will use the back after you choose.

3 · Choose your topic (~5 min)
  • Which map filled in most easily? Who could you actually talk to about it?
  • The choice is yours. Write your topic down.
  • Still torn? Mark the three rings on your two finalists in the next step.

4 · Topic Map Studio: the back side (~15 min)
  • I'll model the back side on my map first. Then you do it for your chosen topic.
  • Circle a few items on your map and note the form each could become.
  • Mark where the evidence sits in the three rings: witnesses (people who know it firsthand — the interviews you conduct), traces (the documentary record — obituary, article, lyrics, label, letter), context (the background world — the history of the place and era).

Purpose: a project built only from the outer ring reads as generic — make sure you have real witnesses and traces, not just context.

5 · Primary source, JSTOR & Gemini Notebook (~25 min)
  • Plan your primary source (5 min)
    • Name the one primary source you'll gather yourself — the interview you'll conduct, the place you'll observe, the artifact you'll collect. Draft the ask or the plan: who, and when. This is on a real clock — line it up today.
  • Search JSTOR (10 min)
    • I'll demo with my JSTOR search for “semicolon”. Then search for your own topic.
    • Look for a peer-reviewed journal article and a long-form work (a book, a long article, a documentary). Not everything labeled a journal article is peer-reviewed. JSTOR is one place to look for a long-form work, not the only one.
  • Set up Gemini Notebook (10 min)
    • I'll demo with my Gemini Notebook. Then create yours and add the sources you have so far. It answers only from what you give it, so its help is only as good as your pile.
    • Ask it what your sources can and cannot answer about your questions.

Purpose: schedule the fieldwork before anything else can crowd it out, and stand up the research home you'll use all month.

Wrap-up & before Monday (5 min):
  • We'll walk through the six parts of the proposal. Most of it grows from today's work. Your source list needs a peer-reviewed article and a long-form work, so keep looking if you haven't found them yet.
  • Finish and submit the proposal at home (due Sun Nov 8, 11:59 p.m.). Keep gathering — and send that interview request.
  • Monday: we interrogate your sources in Gemini Notebook, watch the machine go generic, and start your first genres. Draft due Fri Nov 20.
