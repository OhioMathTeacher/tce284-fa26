The Podcast, Checked

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · what it got right · what it missed

The podcast was made by AI from the project guidelines. The guidelines are the assignment. Where the two disagree, the guidelines win.

What it got right
  • The project is worth 35 points and is due December 9.
  • The Lucky 13 means twelve pieces of your own work, plus required items like the table of contents.
  • The sources: at least one primary source you gather yourself, such as an interview or a firsthand observation, one peer-reviewed journal article, and one long-form work, such as a documentary.
  • Your twelve pieces have to be human and yours.
  • AI gives you the generic version, and your pieces hold the specific details only your sources have.
  • Work made by AI or by other people is welcome icing. It never counts toward the twelve.
  • A scrapbook of others' work is not a project.
  • A quote from someone else inside one of your pieces is fine if it is cited and stays under a tenth of the piece.
  • Raw AI output cannot be your transformed artifact.
  • Lyrics you write count as one of your twelve. The music an AI makes around them does not.

What it missed

What it got wrong or stretched

  • "The whole syllabus centers around this concept called the Lucky 13." And: "the rubric asks for at least one primary source you gather yourself." Both are in the project guidelines, not the syllabus or the rubric.
  • "Oh, no screenshots allowed for full credit." The guidelines say nothing about screenshots. One of their ideas for the AI artifact is "a screen recording of you taking a genAI output apart line by line."
  • "You have to expose what the machine gets wrong." That is one way. The guidelines say "An AI artifact may be admiring or critical." Your move can be "reacted to, taken apart, or recombined."
  • Switching formats is "a brilliant pedagogical trick" that "literally rewires how you understand the topic." The guidelines give no such reason. They ask you to render your research across many forms.

What it never mentioned

  • Six of the eight required pieces. The hosts named the essay and the free verse poem. The others are the Preface, introduction, or Dear Reader, a visual element of your own, unifying elements, note pages, Works Cited, and the Finale.
  • Four more genres of your choosing. Flash fiction, an op-ed, a letter, a recipe, and more.
  • The reflective note. About a page: what AI did in your project, where it helped, where it flattened, what you changed and why.
  • What else does not count. The hosts named only the table of contents. The reflective note and your transformed AI artifact are also required but do not count toward the twelve.
  • The audio and video pieces. 1 to 3 minutes each, made by you, linked from the PDF. They can be among your twelve.
  • How to measure a tenth. Count words in writing, running time in audio and video, and space in an image.
  • Labels. Label every piece with its genre. Label every AI-generated work as AI-made and say what you did with it.
  • Gemini Notebook. Gather your sources into it and use it to interrogate your research.
