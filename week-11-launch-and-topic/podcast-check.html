The Podcast, Checked

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · what it got right · what it missed

The podcast was made by AI from the project guidelines. The guidelines are the assignment. Where the two disagree, the guidelines win.

What it got right
  • The Lucky 13 has thirteen items, but only twelve are pieces. The table of contents, the reflective note and the transformed AI artifact are required, but they do not count toward the twelve.
  • A piece counts when it is your own writing, drawing, footage or voice.
  • A collage cut from magazine photos does not count.
  • Others' work inside one of your pieces has to be cited and stay under about a tenth of the piece.
  • A 400-word op-ed that is half quotation does not count.
  • What you fed an AI is yours. What the AI made is not.
  • Raw AI output earns nothing in the Transformed AI artifact row.
  • Your move on the AI artifact has to be visible.
  • A scrapbook of others' work is not a project.

What it missed

What it got wrong or stretched

  • "The syllabus introduces something called the Lucky 13 Checklist." And: "The rubric demands that you submit 12 pieces of creative work." Both are in the project guidelines, not the syllabus or the rubric.
  • A magazine collage "would actually earn you a zero for that piece." And of the half-quoted op-ed: "you fail that." Neither one counts as one of your twelve pieces. That is not a zero, and it is not a fail. Others' work can still appear in your project, and the guidelines say it "can make the project better."
  • "12 pieces of completely original creation." Not completely. Others' work can appear inside one of your pieces if you cite it and it stays under about a tenth of the piece.
  • "You have to prove you are dominating the tool, tearing it apart and rebuilding it." Taking it apart is one move. The guidelines name three: "reacted to, taken apart, or recombined." They also say "An AI artifact may be admiring or critical."
  • "And never treat the machine as a co-author." The guidelines do not say this. They say "AI can extend or transform your work, but what AI makes is others' work."

What it never mentioned

  • Sources. At least one primary source you gather yourself (an interview, a firsthand observation, an artifact), one peer-reviewed journal article, and one long-form work. Your primary source takes real-world time, so start now.
  • Seven of the eight required pieces. The hosts named only the free verse poem. The others are the Preface, introduction, or Dear Reader, the informational essay (250 to 350 words), a visual element of your own, unifying elements, note pages, Works Cited, and the Finale.
  • The audio and video pieces. 1 to 3 minutes each, made by you, linked from the PDF. They can be among your twelve.
  • How to measure a tenth. Count words in writing, running time in audio and video, and space in an image.
  • The reflective note. About a page: what AI did in your project, where it helped, where it flattened, what you changed and why.
  • Labels. Label every piece with its genre. Label every AI-generated work as AI-made and say what you did with it.
  • Gemini Notebook. Gather your sources into it and use it to interrogate your research.
  • The due date. One PDF via Canvas by Wednesday, December 9 at 11:59 p.m.
