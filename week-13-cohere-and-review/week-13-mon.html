Week 13 · Monday: Making It One Thing

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · note pages · unifying elements · gush · build the draft
Today's goals: Add your note page and your unifying element, the two pieces that make many genres read as one project. Then build Friday's draft.

1 · Note pages (~15 min)
  • Examples (5 min) Three ways to do a note page:
    • Piece by piece: Growing Up Spanglish (page 25). A note at the end with a short entry for each piece: where it came from and why it is there.
    • Numbered notes: Frozen Mourning (pages 3 and 12). “(See note 1)” in the text sends the reader to the Notes.
    • Up front: The Allosaurs in Phoenix (page 4). A handwritten “Explanation” of what is true and what is made up.
  • WWM (5 min)
    • You read WWM ch20, "It Ain't Bourbon Yet": name one piece of yours that is not aged yet, and say what it still needs.
    • You read WWM ch21, "Befriending Revision" and ch22, "Dwelling in Your Words". With a partner: what does Romano mean by befriending revision — and what did Abigail hear when her commentary kept "barking to be groomed"?
  • You (5 min) Mark which of your pieces need a note.

Purpose: let the reader in on the research and the craft without breaking the pieces themselves.

2 · Gush (~10 min)
  • You: gush the note for one piece you marked. Where did it come from? What is fact, and what is invented? What might a reader miss?

3 · Unifying elements (~15 min)
  • Examples (5 min) Three ways to tie a project together:
    • A repeated image: When Life Hands You Lemons (note 2, page 18). Images of a growing plant come back whenever the story shifts.
    • A piece spliced throughout: Calumet Country Club (Endnotes, page 37). A waitress’s “Unofficial Glossary of Terms” is split up and spliced through the packet.
    • A strip on every page: Frozen Mourning (note 3, page 12). A timeline of the sinking runs along the bottom of pages 3 to 10.
  • Think, pair, share (10 min) What unifying element could run through your project?

4 · Work time (~40 min)
  • Your draft is due Fri Nov 20 at 11:59 p.m. (Draft guideline) It needs:
    • The eight required pieces, each one started, in order: preface, introduction, or Dear Reader; the brief informational essay; a free-verse poem; a visual element you made; your unifying element; note page(s); Works Cited; the finale.
    • A writer's note at the front: what feedback you want, and what you are unsure about.
    • One PDF, with each element labeled.
    • Before you submit: put the draft into Gemini Notebook with your sources and ask where it says something your sources don't support.
  • I'll circulate and sign off on unifying elements.
Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
