Week 3 · Wednesday — Voices

Act I · Hearkening to Voices · whose voice do you carry that you don't hear anymore?
Only class this week — Monday, Sept 7 is Labor Day. Voice Print launches, drafts, and heads home to compose today.
Today's goals: Hear how one remembered voice brings a person alive, learn what makes dialog feel real, and launch OP3 · Voice Print — a moment with a voice you don't hear anymore, in pure dialog.

0 · Daily Poem — "Words" (~10 min)
  • Missing Persons, "Words" (1982). Read the lyric, then hear it sung.
  • One voice asking whether anybody on the other end is listening. What does the singing do to those words that the page doesn't?

Purpose: the standing ritual — a voice straining to be heard, on the day we listen for one.

1 · Housekeeping + Writing Place debrief (~5 min)
  • Before you see it: Journaler may notice when you write about things like self-harm and show you counseling numbers. Nobody is notified — not me, not the university — and it never prints on what you submit. Our course site spells out what Journaler does and does not know about you.
  • Did you use one of the same images you shared in class last time?
  • The group share was my attempt to get you paying closer attention to detail — in images, and more broadly in conversations you overhear and in what you read. What new details about your writing space emerged for you through it?
  • Was this assignment difficult? Easy? Say you did this activity with your own students — what would you change about it? What would you keep?

Listen carefully to Romano read "My Father's Voice" on WMUB 88.5, Father's Day 2006 — paying particular attention to any dialog in the recording.

Open after the recording — two passages from the broadcast

"Why I come 'cross?" Uncle Gigi said. "Same reason everybody come 'cross. You make a better living, a better home, a better life."
Both appear in Chapter 9. Can you find them? Is the dialog the same in both? Different?

2b · Daspit — "'None of Us'" (~8 min, pairs)
  • "'None of Us'" — four poems, 2017 to 2020. The last one ends here:
    "if I lost all of this" and he waves his IV and monitor laden arms at the various tubes he's connected to "would I just disappear?" "no dad, you won't disappear none of us in this hospital will. none of us."
    Read the quoted lines in the other three and bring back two: the one that surprised you, and one you don't think is Dad speaking.
  • Whole group. Read your finds aloud. Romano's claim: "We remember the voice, and the person comes alive." Do you buy it?

Purpose: a man built almost entirely out of quoted lines.

3 · What makes dialog real (ch10) (~20 min)
  • Watch two exchanges: Silver Linings Playbook and Birdman. What makes the talk feel real — the fragments, the overlaps, what is not said?
  • A conversation of Romano's own, from Chapter 10 — he ran into a colleague, a math education teacher, at a coffee shop early one morning. Neither speaker is named:
    "Fun? That's easy: a rich mathematical problem!" "Fun for English teachers and their students." "Still easy. I've seen you teach. You love to have fun." "I do. And I want students to enjoy my classes, but I've got some cognitive dissonance." "Yeah?" "Learning to be literate is serious business. I'm uneasy with fun." "Ah! You want fun, but you want deep fun." "Yes! Deep fun! Like deep massage."
    Which one is Romano and which is the math teacher? How can you tell? How do you know when each one starts speaking? And how many dialog tags do you count?
  • Romano's rules for writing dialog, from Chapter 10: exact words, not the gist; simple tags, mostly "said"; no adverbs propping them up; new paragraph per speaker; short exchanges; say it aloud. Do the two clips follow them?

4 · Launch Voice Print + gush the voice (~25 min)
  • Meet OP3 · Voice Print: one moment with a voice you don't hear anymore, in pure dialog.
  • In Journaler, 5-minute timer, gush the exchange — just the two voices, back and forth, the words only they used. Real or imagined. Gush mode locks editing.
  • After the timer, Journaler's reflection partner is optional. If you open it, it asks how the writing went — never what the voice should say. It does not write the dialog for you.

Purpose: get the voices on the page while the sound of them is close.

Wrap-up & at home:
  • Compose OP3 · Voice Print from your gush — one page, pure dialog, simple tags, new paragraph per speaker. Read it aloud to test it. (OP3 due Fri · Canvas.)
  • Turn in a short paragraph with it: why these words, what you left out, and what you want us to hear in the voice. The paragraph is required; the reflection partner is optional.
  • Read J. Edwards, "Beyond Presence" (the Invisibility scene) + WWM ch11 · ch12 · ch13 (through "Make Me Taste It") for Monday.
