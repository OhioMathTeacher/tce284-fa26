Week 12 · Monday: Interrogate & Draft

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · the Question Lab · the Introduction Lab · your first pieces
Today's goals: Finalize topics, ask your sources real questions, gush an introduction (your first required piece).

1 · Question Lab (~35 min)
  • Demo (15 min) I'll ask my Gemini Notebook (Todd link) and a plain chatbot the same question about semicolons: When did the semicolon first become popular among writers? We'll read the two answers together in Side by Side.
  • Your turn (15 min)
    • Tips for posing questions:
      1. Ask a single question (avoid "and" or "also").
      2. Open questions with a factual answer work best.
      3. Ask questions that clearly relate to your topic and your sources.
    • You: load a source into your Gemini Notebook and ask it one question. Ask a plain chatbot the same question. Compare the two answers in Side by Side, with your question in the box at the top.
    • No source yet? Pair with someone who has one.
    • To clear your notebook's chat, click the three-dots menu at the top of the chat and choose Delete chat history.
  • The moral of the story (5 min) what I found.

Purpose: your notebook answers only from the sources you gave it. Use it to find what is already in your pile.

2 · Introduction Lab (~30 min)
  • Examples (15 min)
    • Preface, Introduction, or Dear Reader. All three do the same job. They tell your reader how the project came to be and how to read it. They are different forms. Here is one of each:
      • Preface: Marilyn (page 4). A short note before the work about why the writer made it.
      • Introduction: Third Culture Kid (page 4). A titled essay about how the writer came to the topic.
      • Dear Reader: Inside Out (page 3). A letter to the reader about what is coming and how to read it.
    • The brief informational essay: 250 to 350 words of vivid, straight-ahead writing that boils your topic down to essentials. See the guidelines.
  • Gush and share (15 min) You: gush a brief introduction to your topic. Then share what you gushed about.

Purpose: two of the required pieces, taught quickly, so the rest of class is writing.

3 · Work time (~15 min)
  • A good way to hone in on a topic is to use the Mind Map. For those of you who are still on the fence or haven't submitted your topic proposal, you might have a look at this tool. Even if you've submitted, the deadline isn't until Tuesday at 11:59 p.m. Why not explore the Mind Map? It might help you refine your topic!
  • You: finish your MRP Proposal if it isn't in yet.
  • Or draft your opening piece or your informational essay. Build it from the specific details your sources gave you.
  • Or explore Side by Side with other pairs: your gush, a classmate's, a chatbot's, your notebook's.

Purpose: write your first real pages while your sources are fresh in your mind.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
