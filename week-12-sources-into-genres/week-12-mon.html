Week 12 · Monday: Interrogate & Draft

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · the Gemini Notebook lab · the flattening demo · your first pieces
Today's goals: Use your Gemini Notebook to find what your sources actually say, see what a chatbot writes about your topic, and start your first two required pieces.

1 · Gemini Notebook lab (~20 min)
  • You: add any new sources. Then ask your notebook real questions about your topic: what surprises you, where your sources disagree, and what a claim is based on. Save the useful answers.
  • I'll show one set of my own questions in my Gemini Notebook, then walk around and help.
  • To clear your notebook's chat, click the three-dots menu at the top of the chat and choose Delete chat history.

Purpose: your notebook answers only from the sources you gave it. Use it to find what is already in your pile.

2 · The flattening demo (~15 min)
  • I'll ask a plain chatbot to "write about" a topic, and we'll read the result together. It will be accurate and smooth, and it will say what anyone could say about that topic.
  • We'll put it next to my Gemini Notebook's answer to the same question in Side by Side, and list what the two share and what only one has.
  • You: do the same with your topic. Save what it writes. This generic version is your "before."

Purpose: see the generic version of your topic. Your project has to do what it can't: use the specific details only your sources have.

3 · Mini-lesson: the opening piece and the informational essay (~15 min)
  • Preface, Introduction, or Dear Reader. All three do the same job. They tell your reader how the project came to be and how to read it. They are different forms. Here is one of each:
    • Preface: Marilyn (page 4). A short note before the work about why the writer made it.
    • Introduction: Third Culture Kid (page 4). A titled essay about how the writer came to the topic.
    • Dear Reader: Inside Out (page 3). A letter to the reader about what is coming and how to read it.
  • The brief informational essay: 250 to 350 words of vivid, straight-ahead writing that boils your topic down to essentials. See the guidelines.
  • You: pick which one to start.

Purpose: two of the required pieces, taught quickly, so the rest of class is writing.

4 · Work time (~30 min)
  • You: draft your opening piece or your informational essay. Build it from the specific details your sources gave you, not from the generic version.
  • I'll walk around and help.

Purpose: write your first real pages while your sources are fresh in your mind.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
