Week 12 · Wednesday: Poem, Image & the First AI Move

Act III · Multimodal Research Project · free-verse poem · visual element · the Moves List
Today's goals: Draft a free-verse poem from one concrete detail, plan your visual element, and start your transformed AI artifact.

1 · Mini-lesson: the free-verse poem (~15 min)
  • I'll share a poem I wrote from a node on my Mind Map: No longer free.
  • Remember Romano's mentors chapter, WWM ch19, "Mentors, Imitation, Apprenticeship." My poem is written after King, the way Romano's "Awe" is written after Gendler.
  • The poem criteria in the guidelines: sensory image, specific detail, attention to line and space, precise words.
  • You: draft a poem from one Object or one Moment in your topic, rendered close.
  • Hint: a Mind Map can help. Have you generated one for your sources in Gemini Notebook?

Purpose: the poem grounded in a real particular, not a topic in general.

2 · Mini-lesson: the visual element (~10 min)
  • I'll name the bar: a visual element does something words alone can't.
  • You: plan yours. What image, map, or made object carries meaning in your project?

Purpose: the arts as a load-bearing piece, chosen for payoff.

3 · The Moves List: try one (~30 min)
  • I'll walk the AI Multimodal Moves List and the rule: AI work and others' work can appear, labeled or cited, but never count toward your twelve.
  • You: pick one move and start it. For example: a grounded persona interview, a marked-up draft, a side-by-side against your "before," a data-to-song. Your move has to show.

Purpose: begin the transformed AI artifact now, while there's studio time and I'm in reach.

4 · Work time (~25 min)
  • You read WWM ch20, "It Ain't Bourbon Yet". Romano's bourbon has to sit in the barrel. Before you build: name one piece of yours that is not aged yet, and say what it still needs.
  • You: keep building the poem, the visual, or the AI move. I'll circulate.
Wrap-up & before Monday:
  • Keep drafting toward a real full draft. The draft is due Fri Nov 20, with all core elements present, plus a writer's note.
  • Monday: the note page and the unifying element that make a pile of pieces cohere.
  • Read WWM ch21, "Befriending Revision" · ch22, "Dwelling in Your Words." Monday we make a pile of pieces cohere. These two argue that revision is not a punishment. They also show what it feels like to raise your own standards.
