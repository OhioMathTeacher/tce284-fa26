Martin Luther King: Suspended waiting in Birmingham

From the Mind Map of my semicolons notebook in Gemini Notebook.

The source

But when you have seen vicious mobs lynch your mothers and fathers at will and drown your sisters and brothers at whim; when you have seen hate filled policemen curse, kick and even kill your black brothers and sisters; when you see the vast majority of your twenty million Negro brothers smothering in an airtight cage of poverty in the midst of an affluent society; […] then you will understand why we find it difficult to wait.

Martin Luther King Jr., "Letter from Birmingham Jail," April 16, 1963.

The poem

No longer free

When you are told that you are no longer ableto talk to your friend because he's Chinese;when you're told that your colleagues are now suspects;when you're told to be quiet by those in charge.

The powerlessness, the anger, the sadnessis too much to bear;you begin to understand . . . . .

What it means to be silenced; to be oppressed; to be confined.

Something that used to happen to other people,those on the margins.While I can't claim to understand others' pain or longing,somehow it seems more real to me than before.

I wait for things to change;patiently;but the day never seems to come.

Talking to colleagues in whispers,I know I'm not alone;but I think twice now before I speak;how much longer can I wait?

When we're no longer able to express our feelings to others,we're no longer fully alive—no longer free.

(after Martin Luther King Jr., "Letter from Birmingham Jail")

Romano does this in WWM ch19, "Mentors, Imitation, Apprenticeship": his "Awe" is written after Gendler's The Book of Qualities.
On repeating a pattern ("when… when… when…," "to be… to be… to be…"), see WWM ch11, "Riding the Rhythm of Parallel Structure": parallel structures "lift readers and propel them forward."
