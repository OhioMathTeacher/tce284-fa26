Week 6 · Monday — The Currere Begins

Act II · What is currere · one thread, across your whole life in schools
Today's goals: Name the method you've already practiced five times. Do the four gushes — go back, go forward, look across — and find the one thread that runs through your life in schools.

0 · Daily Poem — "Ethics" (~10 min)

  • Linda Pastan, "Ethics" — read it aloud (or hear Pastan read it herself). Her speaker was asked a question in school she couldn't really answer, and only understood it years later, standing in a museum as an old woman herself.
  • Don't get stuck on "ethics class." When has the meaning of something a teacher asked or said only caught up with you later?

Purpose: that gap — between the moment and its meaning — is what currere is made of. The method is in the room before we name it.

1 · Name the method (~15 min)

  • Act I is behind you. Every One-Pager was a tiny currere — a scene plus a coda reaching across time. This is the full version, sustained on one thread that matters.
  • In pairs, skim the Moore reading ("Starting the Conversation") and look for these four terms: (1) Regressive; (2) Progressive; (3) Analytical; and (4) Synthetical. What does each term mean? Use context clues from the reading to help you.
  • The frame: currere is a thread across past, present, and future. Not one dramatic moment, and not four labeled boxes. The four steps are how you gather. The essay is one woven thing.

Purpose: name what they've been doing, and set the thread-across-time expectation before a word is written.

2 · The four gushes, in Journaler (~25 min)

Timed gush mode — no stopping, no fixing. Gather the material; don't write "the essay" yet.

  • Regressive (~8 min) — go back. Free-associate your life in schools, as far back as you can reach. Teachers, a classroom, a grade on a page, a thing said to you. No order. When the buzzer sounds, note how you feel about what came up.
  • Progressive (~8 min) — go forward. Imagine yourself teaching a year from now, five years from now. Free-associate that future the same way. This one is harder. Let it be.
  • Analytical (~5 min) — lay them side by side. Set past and future next to each other. What runs through both? Name it. Don't decide yet what it means.
  • Synthetical — later. Putting it back together is the work of the coming weeks, not today.

Purpose: generate the raw material for all four moments in one sitting, so the future and the present get written before the vivid past takes over.

3 · From moments to a thread (~20 min)

  • Pull five or six key moments from your life as a teacher and a learner. Concrete scenes, the kind that stay with you.
  • Under each moment, write a possible theme, and how that moment has shaped you up to the present.
  • Now read down your list. Which theme keeps coming back? That recurring theme, running from those moments into who you are becoming, is your thread.

Purpose: start with moments, because that is where real memory lives, then let the thread surface from them instead of forcing it.

4 · Launch + the Scavenger Hunt (~10 min)

  • Meet the Currere Guidelines — the self-study you build from here. The arc is topic conference → draft → draft conference → final draft + audio; each piece has its own one-page guideline (see the assessments overview for points and due dates).
  • Heads-up: the first conference is a topic conference — you'll bring a short packet (a couple of candidate threads, a currere whose form you admire, and Todd-in-a-Can's read of your threads).
Wrap-up & before Wednesday — the Scavenger Hunt:
  • Go out onto the Currere Exchange Journal and find a published currere that pulls you in. This is a real journal you could submit to.
  • Read it twice. On a half sheet, mark how it hits each of the four moments — regressive, progressive, analytical, synthetical. Bring it Wednesday.
