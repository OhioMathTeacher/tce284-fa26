Week 6 · Wednesday — Currere in the Wild

Act II · What is currere · find your one thread
Today's goals: Put your past and future gushes side by side and name the thread that runs through both — the one that reaches back, sits in the present, and leans into your future. Then compare what you found in five real curreres, meet the Guidelines, and start your topic-conference packet.

0 · Past, future, side by side — then your thread (~20 min)

No poem today. Open Journaler: Currere. Monday ended on the progressive; today we finish the job. There's time for everyone to get there, whatever you have from Monday.

  • On your own (~10 min). Two ways in:
    • If you're done with gushes 1 and 2 and want to keep them, go straight to Examine the in-between. Your two gushes come up in facing panes. In the box underneath, name what runs through both. Not what it means — what recurs.
    • If you don't have the gushes, or want to start over, gush them now, 5 minutes each — set the timer to 5:00 with −. Regressive first, then progressive built out of it. Skip Romano's question today. Starting over? Click ＋ Add to notebook on the old gush first: starting a new one replaces what's saved there.
  • Whole group (~4 min). Three or four people who worked the in-between: what showed up in both, and how did you spot it? What did you do when nothing matched? If you just finished gushing, listen for how they found it, not what they found. That's your next step.
  • On your own again (~6 min).
    • Just gushed? Now go to Examine the in-between and name what runs through both.
    • Already there? Pick the one that pulls hardest, then start a second candidate thread. Your conference packet asks for two or three.
  • Everyone, before block 1: ＋ Add to notebook on your in-between page, then name your thread with ＋ New thread… and put the chip on.

Purpose: the analytical is the next move after Monday, and what recurs across past and future is the thread. Nobody is made to redo Monday, and anyone who missed it catches up in the same block. The mid-block share lets the people who have already compared show the rest how the comparing goes — the how, not the answer, so nobody borrows a thread. Starting with their own writing also means the published pieces in block 1 get read by people who have just done the thing.

1 · What you found in the five (~20 min)
  • Hands up (~1 min). Who found all four in the first piece? Who had to move to a second? A third?
  • Group up (~1 min). One corner per piece, by whichever of the five you worked on. Half sheets out. Didn't get to it? Join any corner and read along.
  • What we agree on (~6 min). Each corner reads out the lines where your read and mine agree, and says what makes them that kind of attention. Where we part, bring it to your topic conference.
  • Nobody read these looking for the moments the first time through. If a moment was hard to find, that is a fact about the piece, not about you.
  • The finding (~6 min). Across the five: only "Beyond Presence" is built as four sections that match the four moments, and it is the only one in which the writer imagines a future. Four never look ahead in the writer's own voice — Daspit and O'Hara contain no future-tense sentence at all — and neither does Pastan's poem. Every one was published. On Monday you wrote the moment four of these five skipped.
  • So is a piece missing a moment still a currere? Settle it from the sources, not from me. Moore never describes four parts of an essay — he says "method," "process," "four-step," and his own directions are headed Stage 1 – Regression through Stage 4 – Synthesis, with the writing coming after the four stages. The journal's call asks for work that "explore[s] the Currere process" and welcomes "poetry" and "experimental pieces." The moments are what the writer does; the essay is the trace.
  • The harder question (~4 min): what does a piece lose without it? O'Hara's last poem, read aloud. Eight poems about having watched, and nothing in them is becoming anything. Sit with that before answering.
  • Which is why your guidelines ask you for the progressive: you are learning the method, not publishing an artifact.
  • Two minutes on the Currere Exchange Journal itself — run out of Miami, and the current issue is twenty-two pieces on scripted curriculum. Who writes here? What would it take for a piece of yours to belong?

Purpose: they have read these five, so checking their own read against mine is closer work than hunting a sixth piece — and the missing progressive shows up as a pattern rather than a rule. Agreement only: an open round of disagreement can eat the class, and the finding answers most of it anyway.

2 · Launch the currere and walk the Guidelines (~15 min)
  • The launch (~4 min). The arc is topic conference → draft → draft conference → final draft + audio; each piece has its own one-page guideline. Final draft is due Friday, October 30. Two shapes, both from pieces you've read: an open currere breaks into named parts that walk the four moments (J. Edwards, "Beyond Presence"); a braided one runs as one piece and holds its meaning until late (M. T. Edwards, "Who's Cheating Whom?"). Neither is right. Let your thread decide.
  • With a partner (~6 min), read the Currere Guidelines and skim Deliverables, then write down every question a careful person should be able to answer: When is it due? How many drafts? How long? What is it actually about? How is it graded?
  • Answers together (~5 min). Every question, out loud, as a class — including the two one-on-one conferences and the author's audio at the end.

Purpose: the launch Monday didn't reach, folded into the read-through. This is the block that gives if the clock runs short — the Guidelines can be reread at home; the other three can't.

3 · Start your topic-conference packet (~20 min)
  • The packet (~5 min). Your topic conference is one-on-one on Zoom — Monday Oct 5 or Tuesday Oct 6, whichever slot you book. No whole-class meeting Monday. Before it, you turn in the Conference Packet: two or three candidate threads, a model currere, and Todd-in-a-Can's read of your threads. Due Sunday night, October 4.
  • Book your slot now (~2 min), before anything else: Currere Topic Conference booking.
  • Work (~13 min). You already have one thread from block 0. Write a second candidate, a sentence or two each. Then paste the Todd-in-a-Can prompt from the end of the packet into whatever model you use, paste your threads under it, and read what comes back.

Purpose: leave with a booked slot, two threads, and one Todd-in-a-Can response — most of the packet — so Sunday is finishing, not starting. The four blocks add up to 75; the last five minutes are unassigned on purpose.
