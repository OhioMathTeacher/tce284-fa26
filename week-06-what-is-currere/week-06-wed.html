2 · What you found in the five (~25 min)
  • Hands up (~1 min). Who found all four in the first piece? Who needed a second? A third? A movement that was hard to find is a fact about the piece, not about you.
  • Count off (~1 min). “You've all looked over curreres again with a sharper eye for the four movements. Now we're going to count off, and you'll have some time with classmates to look for each.”
  • Groups (~5 min). Which piece did each of you read, and where did you find each movement? Point at lines. Sheet stays closed.
  • The sheet (~5 min). Now open the four-movements sheet. Compare what your group found against my read: where do we agree, and where don't we?
  • Debrief (~5 min). Whole group. Each group names one place their read matched mine and one place it didn't. Disagreements go to your topic conference.
  • The finding (~5 min). Only "Beyond Presence" imagines a future. Four of the five never look ahead, and all five were published. On Monday you wrote the movement they skipped. Still currere? Moore calls it a method, a "four-step process": the movements are what the writer does; the essay is the trace.
  • The harder question (~3 min). What does a piece lose without the progressive? Read O'Hara's last poem aloud. That's why your guidelines ask for it.

  • Demo threads to paste (made up, not a student's) 
    1. Finishing first. In elementary school I was always done early, so I got handed another worksheet. I learned that fast meant smart. In my field placement this fall I keep calling on whoever raises a hand first, and I'm not sure what that means for the kids who are still thinking.

2. Mrs. Hall, my fourth-grade teacher. She read aloud to us every day after lunch, and she's the reason I love reading.

3. Phones in the classroom. They are a huge distraction, and schools need clearer policies about them.
    • Each one is built to meet a different one of Todd-in-a-Can's three questions. 1 moves across time and isn't settled: past, present placement, an open question. 2 is real and warm but lives only in the past. 3 is a topic anyone could write, not a thread anyone lived.
    • Before you read the reply out, ask the room which one they'd bet it favors, and why. Then see if it agrees.
