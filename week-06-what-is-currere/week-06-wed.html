Week 6 · Wednesday — Currere in the Wild

Act II · What is currere · find your one thread
Today's goals: Argue about how five real writers built theirs, see the journal you could publish in, and commit to the one thread you'll follow — the one that reaches back, sits in the present, and leans into your future.

0 · Daily Poem — "Tell all the truth but tell it slant" (~10 min)

  • Emily Dickinson, "Tell all the truth but tell it slant". Read it aloud. What does she mean by telling the truth "slant" instead of straight out? When have you told something true by coming at it sideways?

Purpose: a currere tells a true thing slant — through scene, not statement. The poem sets that up before we ask them to do it.

1 · What you found, and what you couldn't (~25 min)

  • Half sheets out. Group up by which of the five pieces you worked on — one corner per piece. How many of you had to move to a second piece? A third?
  • Round one: read out the lines where you and I agree, and say what makes them that kind of attention.
  • Round two, the useful one: where did we disagree, or where could you find nothing at all? Bring that to the room and make me defend my answer.
  • Nobody read these looking for the moments the first time through. If a moment was hard to find, that is a fact about the piece, not about you.
  • Across the five: only "Beyond Presence" is built as four sections that match the four moments, and it is the only one of the five in which the writer imagines a future. Four of them never look ahead in the writer's own voice — Daspit and O'Hara contain no future-tense sentence at all — and neither does Pastan's poem. Every one was published.
  • So what is the progressive actually for, if published writers skip it? (It is the one you are least likely to have material for. That is what Monday's second gush was.)
  • Two minutes on the Currere Exchange Journal itself — run out of Miami, and the current issue is twenty-two pieces on scripted curriculum. Who writes here? What would it take for a piece of yours to belong?

Purpose: they have read these five, so checking their own read against mine is closer work than hunting a sixth piece — and the missing progressive shows up as a pattern rather than a rule.

2 · Walk the Guidelines (~15 min)

  • With a partner, read the Currere Guidelines and skim the assessments overview, then write down every question a careful person should be able to answer: When is it due? How many drafts? How long? What is it actually about? How is it graded?
  • Answer them together as a class. Note the two one-on-one conferences and the author's audio at the end.

3 · Pick your thread (~20 min)

  • Monday you named candidates. Today one of them gets chosen. In Journaler, Notebook → What keeps coming back shows you every thread chip you have made and how many pages sit under each. The count is not the answer, but it is evidence.
  • Set your gushes, your moments page, and the piece you reread side by side. Which thread keeps showing up?
  • Write for a few minutes toward the one that pulls hardest. Test it against time: does it reach into your past, sit in your present, and lean toward the teacher you're becoming? If it only lives in the past, keep looking.

Purpose: commit to a thread that can carry all four moments, before a single scene gets drafted.

4 · Set up the topic conference (~10 min)

  • Monday is your topic conference — one-on-one, no whole-class meeting. Come ready to talk your thread through with me.
  • What to build first: the prep packet (below).
Wrap-up & before Monday — your topic-conference packet:
  • Two or three candidate threads, a sentence or two each.
  • A currere whose shape you admire — one of the five works, or anything from the journal — and a line on what you'd borrow from it.
  • Run your threads past Todd-in-a-Can first: which one reaches across past, present, and future? Paste what it says into your packet.
  • Where is Todd-in-a-Can? It is not a button — it is the prompt printed at the end of the Conference Packet. Copy it into whatever model you use, paste your threads under it, and go. It asks questions and tells you what it notices; it will not pick for you and it will not write any of your currere.
