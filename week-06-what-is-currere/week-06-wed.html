Week 6 · Wednesday — Currere in the Wild

Act II · What is currere · find your one thread
Today's goals: Put your past and future gushes side by side and name the thread that runs through both — the one that reaches back, sits in the present, and leans into your future. Then meet the Guidelines, compare what you found in five real curreres, and start your topic-conference packet.

No poem today. Open Journaler: Currere. Monday ended on the progressive; today we finish the job. There's time for everyone to get there, whatever you have from Monday.

1 · Launch the currere and walk the Guidelines (~15 min)
  • The launch (~4 min). A word first: Pinar’s method is usually described in moments or steps, and Moore calls them stages. I call them movements, because they run into one another like the movements of a piece of music. The arc is topic conference → draft → draft conference → final draft + audio; each piece has its own one-page guideline. Final draft is due Friday, October 30. Two shapes, both from pieces you've read: an open currere breaks into named parts that walk the four movements (J. Edwards, "Beyond Presence"); a braided one runs as one piece and holds its meaning until late (M. T. Edwards, "Who's Cheating Whom?"). Neither is right. Let your thread decide.
  • With a partner (~6 min), read the Currere Guidelines and skim Deliverables, then write down every question a careful person should be able to answer: When is it due? How many drafts? How long? What is it actually about? How is it graded?
  • Answers together (~5 min). Every question, out loud, as a class — including the two one-on-one conferences and the author's audio at the end.

2 · What you found in the five (~25 min)
  • Hands up (~1 min). Who found all four in the first piece? Who needed a second? A third? A movement that was hard to find is a fact about the piece, not about you.
  • Corners (~1 min). One per piece of the five. Half sheets out. Didn't get to it? Join any corner.
  • Partners (~10 min). In your corner, walk the piece against the four-movements sheet: what did each of you find? First to cut if we're behind.
  • What we agree on (~5 min). Each corner reads the lines where their read and mine agree. Disagreements go to your topic conference.
  • The finding (~5 min). Only "Beyond Presence" imagines a future. Four of the five never look ahead, and all five were published. On Monday you wrote the movement they skipped. Still currere? Moore calls it a method, a "four-step process": the movements are what the writer does; the essay is the trace.
  • The harder question (~3 min). What does a piece lose without the progressive? Read O'Hara's last poem aloud. That's why your guidelines ask for it.

3 · Start your topic-conference packet (~20 min)
  • The packet (~4 min). Your topic conference is one-on-one on Zoom — Monday Oct 5 or Tuesday Oct 6, whichever slot you book. No whole-class meeting Monday. Before it, you turn in the Conference Packet: two or three candidate threads, a model currere, and Todd-in-a-Can's read of your threads. Due Sunday night, October 4.
  • Book your slot now (~2 min), before anything else: Currere Topic Conference booking.
  • Todd-in-a-Can, live (~4 min). On the screen: open the Conference Packet, copy the prompt at the end, paste it into Gemini, paste a couple of threads under it, and read what comes back together. Use the prompt at the end of the packet — the one at the end of the Guidelines is for your final draft, later. Notice what it does: it asks, it notices, it doesn't pick for you.
  • Demo threads to paste (made up, not a student's) 
    1. Finishing first. In elementary school I was always done early, so I got handed another worksheet. I learned that fast meant smart. In my field placement this fall I keep calling on whoever raises a hand first, and I'm not sure what that means for the kids who are still thinking.

2. Mrs. Hall, my fourth-grade teacher. She read aloud to us every day after lunch, and she's the reason I love reading.

3. Phones in the classroom. They are a huge distraction, and schools need clearer policies about them.
    • Each one is built to meet a different one of Todd-in-a-Can's three questions. 1 moves across time and isn't settled: past, present placement, an open question. 2 is real and warm but lives only in the past. 3 is a topic anyone could write, not a thread anyone lived.
    • Before you read the reply out, ask the room which one they'd bet it favors, and why. Then see if it agrees.
  • Your turn (~10 min). You already have one thread from block 0. Write a second candidate, a sentence or two each. Then run them through Todd-in-a-Can the same way and read what comes back.
