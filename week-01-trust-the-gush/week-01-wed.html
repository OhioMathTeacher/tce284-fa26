Week 1 · Wednesday — Read it, then write it

Act I · Trust the Gush · what happens when you outrun the editor?
Today's goals: Open with a poem, talk through Edwards and the question of who's cheating whom when a machine writes for you, meet the One-Pager, and draft your first one — Why You Write — trusting the gush, in the room.

0 · Daily Poem — "We Wear the Mask" (~15 min)

  • Paul Laurence Dunbar, "We Wear the Mask" (Dayton, Ohio). Read it aloud.
  • What do you notice — the sounds, the repeated lines, the "we"? Who is the "we"? What is the mask, and who is it for?
  • Where have you worn one — as a student, as a future teacher? What does the mask cost, and what does it protect?

Purpose: the standing ritual — start inside real language; today the mask opens the day's question of what we hide and what is real, before Edwards.

1 · Housekeeping (~10 min)

  • Syllabus questions, comments, concerns.
  • Journaler check — is everyone set up from Monday: Groq key in Settings, and the chapters loaded in Readings?

2 · Reading discussion — Edwards (~20 min)

  • In pairs (~10): from "Who's Cheating Whom?" — (a) one idea you agreed with, (b) one you're not sure about, (c) one question you'd ask the writer. Then the move we'll watch in every writer we read this term: mark one place the writer goes back into a real moment and one place they step back to say what it means now.
  • Whole group (~10): share, then the question the piece raises — who's cheating whom when the machine writes for you? We put it on the table and leave it open.

Purpose: work the reading through a task, not a lecture — let the AI question enter in a real writer's words, and start the one lens we bring to every model writer: a real past moment, and the meaning made of it now.

3 · Launch the One-Pager (~15 min)

  • What is a One-Pager? Show Cognition, Craft, and the Voice of the One-Pager — a student's explanation of the form, with five real examples (prose, poem-beside-essay, image-and-text). One page, ~500–600 words, personal, image and text welcome, poem or prose — built around your own writing and the week's reading.
  • This week's One-Pager: Why You Write — why have you written, why do you write? Draft it by trusting the gush (WWM ch2): fast, unedited, no fixing. Submission is in Canvas.

Purpose: you can't make a One-Pager you've never seen. Show the form and a real student's, then name the week's writing move.

4 · Draft OP1 — the gush, in Journaler (~20 min)

  • In the app, start the 5-minute timer and gush on the question: why have you written, why do you write? Gush mode locks editing — no stopping, no fixing. If you stall, write that you've stalled and keep going.
  • When the timer ends, answer the app's few questions about how it went. Then start shaping it into your one-pager — image and text. Optional quick share of one line.

Purpose: the first raw pass, written live in the app — low-stakes practice, and proof the writing is yours before you build the page.

Wrap-up & at home:
  • Finish composing your One-Pager on why you write — one page, image and text. (OP1 due Fri · Canvas.)
  • Read Wiederhold, "How to Read a Blank Page of Writing" (Currere Exchange Journal) + WWM ch3–4 (pp. 9–22, in Canvas Files) for Monday.
