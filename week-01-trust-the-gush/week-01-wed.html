Week 1 · Wednesday — Read it, then write it

Act I · Trust the Gush · what happens when you outrun the editor?
Today's goals: Put Edwards and Romano beside each other, see what keeping a reading looks like, then draft OP1 — Why You Write — trusting the gush, in the room.

0 · Daily Poem — "We Wear the Mask" (~5 min)

  • Paul Laurence Dunbar, "We Wear the Mask" (Dayton, Ohio). Read aloud.
  • What do you notice? Who is the "we"? What does the mask cost, what does it protect?

Purpose: start inside real language — and the mask is Romano's question too: who gets to speak, and what do we hide?

1 · Housekeeping & the Writer's Notebook (~10 min)

  • Syllabus questions, comments, concerns.
  • The Writer's Notebook is now published in Canvas — 50 points, a quarter of the grade. The only part of this course graded on practice, not polish: nothing gets red-penned, spelling doesn't count. Expect 20 or more entries — typically one per class meeting.
  • You keep it in Journaler — it dates and numbers entries and builds the Contents list for you. Read the guidelines this week.
  • Journaler check: Groq key in Settings, chapters loaded in Readings.
  • If you loaded chapters before today, load them again. The files in Canvas now carry the book's own page numbers, so Journaler shows Page 23 where the book says 23, and a passage you keep is cited that way. An older copy still works — it just counts pages from the start of the chapter, so your citations won't match the book or each other.

2 · The readings, and how we keep them (~25 min)

  • Romano first. ch1 Who Are You to Presume to Write? — the permission question, same as the mask. ch2 Trust the Gush — the method for the next 40 minutes.
  • Edwards. In pairs: one idea you agreed with, one you're unsure about, one question for the writer. Then whole group — who's cheating whom when the machine writes for you? On the table, left open.
  • Some notes I took. In Journaler: kept passages, a question I asked Romano, the dated entry. Marking a passage keeps it and cites it, but it is not an entry yet — an entry is what you write about it. My Progress lists each reading that is waiting on you, and you write there. Fine if you haven't started — nothing is collected until December.
  • Write it down, together. Freewrite → For the notebook → Week 1. Monday you gushed about why you write. Romano's plate reads Write 2 — write to express, to communicate, to clarify, to learn. Did you include each of these in your gush? Any reasons Romano didn't have? Keep it — keeping tags it as one of your four required entries.

Purpose: discuss the readings as readings, then show what keeping one looks like — the app in service of the reading, not as a demo.

3 · OP1 — gush, then shape (~40 min)

  • See the form. Cognition, Craft, and the Voice of the One-Pager — five real student examples. One page, ~500–600 words, image and text welcome, prose or poem.
  • The hinge. You already gushed on Monday — today's tip in the app says the polished copy is what makes you think you can't write. Romano says trust the gush. You trusted it; now shape it.
  • Shape it. Open OP1, read your gush back, keep the two or three lines that are alive, cut the rest and build around them — image and text. We work in the app together; ask as you go.
  • Your gush rides along on sheet two of the One-Pager PDF, so it isn't a notebook entry — the Week 1 page you just wrote is.
  • Optional quick share of one line.

Purpose: the One-Pager was introduced Monday, so today is the making. Features surface as they are needed — timer and edit-lock, keeping a passage, the AI-use log, the one-page export.
