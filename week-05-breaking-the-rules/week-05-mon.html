Week 5 · Monday — Break the Rules

Act I · Grammar B · "Who says?"
Today's goals: Learn to be bad on purpose. Try the four Grammar B moves — fragments, labyrinthine sentences, purposeful misspelling, double voice — and launch Breaking the Rules.

0 · Daily Poem — "Short Order Cook" (~10 min)

  • Jim Daniels, "Short Order Cook". Read it aloud. Where does the poem move fast, break, and refuse to sit still? What rules is it not following — and why does it work?

Purpose: the standing ritual — and a poem that already breaks rules in style, so the move is in the room before we name it.

1 · Housekeeping + Show, Don't Tell debrief (~5 min)

  • One line from your Show, Don't Tell that made someone taste it.

2 · Reading discussion — O'Hara + Lamott (~15 min)

  • O'Hara, "Moviehouse Poems" (CEJ), in pairs: where does the language play — sound, line breaks, surprise? Mark one spot that "breaks a rule" and lands.
  • Lamott, "Shitty First Drafts": what permission is she giving? How does "let it be bad" connect to breaking rules on purpose?

Purpose: prime the mindset — play, permission, "be bad now and then" — before we get specific.

3 · The four Grammar B moves (~40 min · ~10 each)

For each move: look at the Grammar B examples together, name what it's doing, then take a 2-minute try in your notebook.

  • Sentence fragments — isolate an idea and let it land. (See "Cat" and the 1913 list-poem.)
  • Labyrinthine sentence — one long, weaving, controlled sentence for something that goes on and on; you hold the period. (See "Waiting for the Bus"; Wolfe's Right Stuff.)
  • Purposeful misspelling — bend a word so the spelling itself carries meaning: seven daze of onederful sites. (See "Spring Break"; Romano's "Day School Gives Out.")
  • Double voice — two voices at once, side by side. (See the two-voice piece; Fleischman's "Fireflies.")

Purpose: hands-on reps at each move, so the one-pager is a choice among tools you've felt, not a leap.

4 · Launch Breaking the Rules (~10 min)

  • Meet Breaking the Rules: a one-pager that uses at least two of these moves, on purpose. Every "error" is one you meant.
  • Heads-up for Wednesday: you'll draft it, then feed it to the machine and watch it get "corrected" — the Generation Loss lab. Then we close Act I by looking back across all five one-pagers.
Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
  • Collect rule-breaking you find in the wild — a sign, a lyric, a text — and bring one. Come with a subject that matters to you to break rules on.
