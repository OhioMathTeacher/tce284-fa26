Week 5 · Wednesday — Draft it, flatten it, look back

Act I finale · Generation Loss · what does the machine "correct" away — and what keeps showing up across your five pages?
Last day of Act I. You draft the rule-breaking piece, watch the machine flatten it, then look back across all five one-pagers for the thread that starts your currere in Act II.
Today's goals: Draft Breaking the Rules, run it through the machine and watch it normalize to the mean, then reread your five one-pagers and name the thread that keeps returning — your candidate currere.

0 · Daily Poem — "O Me! O Life!" (~10 min)

  • Walt Whitman, "O Me! O Life!". Read it aloud. After all the doubt and the "empty and useless years," Whitman's answer: "that the powerful play goes on, and you may contribute a verse." On the last day of Act I — after the machine flattened your words — what is the verse that only you can add?

Purpose: the standing ritual — and the note Act I ends on: the play goes on, and your verse is the un-fakeable thing you bring to it.

1 · Housekeeping (~5 min)

  • Bring your rule-breaking-in-the-wild find? Share one. Questions on finishing Act I.

2 · Draft Breaking the Rules in Journaler (~20 min)

  • Start the 5-minute timer and gush on something that matters to you — breaking the rules on purpose (at least two of the four moves). Gush mode locks editing, so break freely.
  • Then shape it a little on the page. Keep it broken; every "error" is one you meant. This is your Breaking the Rules draft — and pass zero for the lab.

Purpose: get a genuinely rule-broken piece down — the more Grammar B, the more the lab will show.

3 · Generation Loss experiment (~25 min)

  • Your Grammar B piece is pass zero. Ask the machine to "clean this up and correct it" → pass one. Run the same on that. Keep going to pass ten. Save passes 0 / 1 / 5 / 10 side by side.
  • Watch it: fragments become sentences, wisdoom becomes wisdom, the labyrinth untangles, the double voice merges into one.
  • Reflect (in Journaler): What did the machine fix first? What is gone by pass ten? When did it stop being yours?

Purpose: feel, don't just hear, the machine's pull to the mean — and locate exactly what only you make. This is the hinge into the currere.

4 · The Look-Back (~20 min)

  • Reread all five one-pagers — Why You Write, Writing Place, Voice Print, Show Don't Tell, Breaking the Rules. The Look-Back: what keeps showing up? Across the place, the voices, the moment, the rules you break — name the thread that recurs.
  • Write one short entry naming that thread. It's a candidate for your currere — the longer self-study you begin in Act II.

Purpose: five weeks of small pieces were laying down material — today you find the line running through them, and Act I hands off to Act II.

Wrap-up & at home:
  • Finish Breaking the Rules (keep it broken) and export a one-page PDF with your Generation Loss reflection. (Due Fri · Canvas.)
  • Bring your currere thread to Week 6, and read Moore, "Starting the Conversation" (the currere how-to). It's where your currere starts.
