Week 5 · Wednesday — Gush it, flatten it, find the thread

Act I finale · Generation Loss · what does the machine "correct" away — and what keeps coming back across your pages?
Last day of Act I. You gush a rule-breaking page, watch the machine flatten it, then reread your one-pagers for the first thread — the one that starts your currere in Act II. The real Breaking the Rules page gets written at home, with time, and goes through the machine again.
Today's goals: Meet Breaking the Rules, gush a practice page and run it through the machine — watch it normalize to the mean — then reread your one-pagers and name the thread that keeps returning: your candidate currere.

0 · Daily Poem — "O Me! O Life!" (~10 min)
  • Walt Whitman, "O Me! O Life!". Read it aloud. After all the doubt and the "empty and useless years," Whitman's answer: "that the powerful play goes on, and you may contribute a verse." Hold onto that line. In an hour the machine will have flattened your page — and the question will be: what is the verse that only you can add?

Purpose: the standing ritual — and the note Act I ends on: the play goes on, and your verse is the un-fakeable thing you bring to it. We come back to it at the end, when you name your first thread.

1 · Housekeeping + meet Breaking the Rules (~10 min)
  • Bring your rule-breaking-in-the-wild find? Share one.
  • Open Breaking the Rules together: a one-pager that uses at least two of Monday's moves, on purpose. Every "error" is one you meant. Then the machine gets it — that's step 4. Read the assessment table; ask now what's unclear.

2 · Gush it — the practice page (~10 min)
  • Freewrite/OPs → 5 · Breaking the Rules. Leave the clock at 8:00 and Start the gush on something that matters to you — breaking the rules on purpose (at least two of the four moves). Gush mode locks editing, so break freely. Said it before the clock runs out? ■ Done — stop the clock freezes it right there.
  • Then Shape it a little in the right-hand column — two minutes, no more. Keep it broken. This is not your One-Pager; it's the practice page, and it's pass zero for today's lab. The rougher the better: more for the machine to "fix."
  • No "Reflecting on Writing" on this one — One-Pager 5's reflection comes after the machine has had its turn (step 4).

Purpose: get a genuinely rule-broken page down fast, so the lab has something to flatten. The real page comes later, with time.

3 · Chiang — "ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web" (~10 min)
  • The photocopier (the article, if you need it open). A German construction company copies a floor plan; the rooms are 14.13, 21.11 and 17.42 square metres; on the copy all three say 14.13. The copy looked right. What made that worse than a blurry copy?
  • His claim: "Think of ChatGPT as a blurry jpeg of all the text on the Web… the blurriness occurs in a way that doesn't make the picture as a whole look less sharp." Say it back in your own words. What's the "blur" — and why don't you notice it?
  • The lab, in his words: "Repeatedly resaving a jpeg creates more compression artifacts, because more information is lost every time. It's the digital equivalent of repeatedly making photocopies of photocopies." That's what you're about to do to your own page, twenty times.
  • Monday's Lamott, from the other side: "Your first draft isn't an unoriginal idea expressed clearly; it's an original idea expressed poorly." Pick one thing on your page you'd bet is un-blurrable — a fragment, a respelling, a line break. Put a finger on it. In a minute you'll find out.

Purpose: name the idea before the lab makes it visible, so what the strip shows has a word waiting for it.

4 · Generation Loss — Telefone (~25 min)
  • Telephone, except the machine is every player. Under your two columns, band 3 · Generation Loss — play Telefone: press Send this page to Telefone →. Today's round is on the practice page; at home you'll do it again on the finished one, and both print with your submission. That opens the Telefone tab (after Notebook) with your shaped page as Pass 0 · yours. No AI connected yet? Change model opens ⚙ Settings → AI — Groq is the one to pick.
  • Ask it to — three requests, all ticked: clean it up & correct it · fix grammar, spelling, punctuation · rewrite in clear, standard English. Press AI Revise: the machine gets your page and those requests, and what comes back pops up as Pass 1. Press it again — pass two is made from pass one, not from yours. Then ×10 and watch the tabs fill (it stops at 20).
  • Read the strip. Under each pass is how much of your page is still there. Where does it stop dropping? That flat line is the machine's idea of a finished page — Chiang's blurry JPEG, except the picture is yours and you can watch the detail go. Under the writing, switch on Show what this pass changed and go back to Pass 1: red is what it cut, green is what it put in. What went first?
  • Watch it: fragments become sentences, wisdoom becomes wisdom, the labyrinth untangles, the double voice merges into one — and the line breaks close up.
  • Save round — it keeps Pass 0 beside the last pass, with the model, the count and the date, in the Rounds list below. That's round 1, the gush. If there's time, Start over, untick two of the requests or Change model, and play the same page again.
  • Start the reflection in the Generation Loss reflection box under your rounds (it's the same box back on One-Pager 5, under band 3): What did the machine fix first? What is gone by the end that was there at the start? What refused to go? You'll finish it at home, once the finished page has been through too.

Purpose: feel, don't just hear, the machine's pull to the mean — and locate exactly what only you make. This is the hinge into the currere.

5 · The first thread (~15 min)
  • What a thread is. From the Writer's Notebook guidelines: a thread is anything that keeps coming back. Your grandmother. The blank page. A room you keep describing. A question you cannot leave alone. You name it, and you put that name on every entry it turns up in. One entry can't show you a preoccupation; six of them, held together, can. Before the notebook is turned in, you'll pick one thread and write your reading of it — that's a requirement, and Journaler's Threads view has a panel, What keeps coming back, to help you find one.
  • Find your first one now. Reread your one-pagers — Why You Write, Writing Place, Voice Print, Show Don't Tell, and today's gush. Across the place, the voices, the moment, the rules you broke: what keeps showing up?
  • Write one short entry in your Writer's Notebook naming it, and tag it with that name. It counts as an entry, it's the first thread in your notebook, and it's a candidate for your currere — the longer self-study you begin in Act II. Whitman's question from this morning, now that the machine has had its turn: what's the verse it couldn't take?

Purpose: five weeks of small pieces were laying down material — today you name the line running through them, and Act I hands off to Act II.

Wrap-up & at home:
  • Now write the real one. Take your time with Breaking the Rules — one page, at least two moves, every "error" one you meant. Today's gush can be the seed or you can start fresh. Then do to it what we did in class: Send this page to Telefone →, play it through, Save round. That's round 2, the finished page. Now finish the reflection with both rounds in front of you: did the finished page lose more than the gush, or less? What survived both? And what does that mean for your writing — and for the writing of your students?
  • At the bottom of One-Pager 5 press Export One-Pager (PDF). One PDF: your One-Pager, your writing session and AI-use log, your reflection, and both rounds. That's the whole submission. (Due Fri · Canvas.)
  • Bring your currere thread to Week 6, and read Moore, "Starting the Conversation" (the currere how-to). It's where your currere starts.
