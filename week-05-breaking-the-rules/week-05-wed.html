Week 5 · Wednesday — Draft it, flatten it, look back

Act I finale · Generation Loss · what does the machine "correct" away — and what keeps showing up across your five pages?
Last day of Act I. You draft the rule-breaking piece, watch the machine flatten it, then look back across all five one-pagers for the thread that starts your currere in Act II.
Today's goals: Draft Breaking the Rules, run it through the machine and watch it normalize to the mean, then reread your five one-pagers and name the thread that keeps returning — your candidate currere.

0 · Daily Poem — "O Me! O Life!" (~10 min)
  • Walt Whitman, "O Me! O Life!". Read it aloud. After all the doubt and the "empty and useless years," Whitman's answer: "that the powerful play goes on, and you may contribute a verse." Hold onto that line. In an hour the machine will have flattened your page — and the question will be: what is the verse that only you can add?

Purpose: the standing ritual — and the note Act I ends on: the play goes on, and your verse is the un-fakeable thing you bring to it. We come back to it in the Look-Back.

1 · Housekeeping (~5 min)
  • Bring your rule-breaking-in-the-wild find? Share one. Questions on finishing Act I.

2 · Draft Breaking the Rules in Journaler (~15 min)
  • Freewrite/OPs → 5 · Breaking the Rules. Set the clock to 5:00 and Start the gush on something that matters to you — breaking the rules on purpose (at least two of the four moves). Gush mode locks editing, so break freely. Said it before the clock runs out? ■ Done — stop the clock freezes it right there.
  • Then Shape it a little in the right-hand column. Keep it broken; every "error" is one you meant. This is your Breaking the Rules draft — and pass zero for Telefone.
  • No "Reflecting on Writing" on this one — One-Pager 5's reflection comes after the machine has had its turn (step 4).

Purpose: get a genuinely rule-broken piece down — the more Grammar B, the more the lab will show.

3 · Chiang, briefly — "ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web" (~5 min)
  • The photocopier (the article, if you need it open). A German construction company copies a floor plan; the rooms are 14.13, 21.11 and 17.42 square metres; on the copy all three say 14.13. The copy looked right. What made that worse than a blurry copy?
  • His claim: "Think of ChatGPT as a blurry jpeg of all the text on the Web… the blurriness occurs in a way that doesn't make the picture as a whole look less sharp." Say it back in your own words. What's the "blur" — and why don't you notice it?
  • The lab, in his words: "Repeatedly resaving a jpeg creates more compression artifacts, because more information is lost every time. It's the digital equivalent of repeatedly making photocopies of photocopies." That's what you're about to do to your own page, twenty times.
  • Monday's Lamott, from the other side: "Your first draft isn't an unoriginal idea expressed clearly; it's an original idea expressed poorly." Pick one thing on your page you'd bet is un-blurrable — a fragment, a respelling, a line break. Put a finger on it. In a minute you'll find out.

Purpose: name the idea before the lab makes it visible, so what the strip shows has a word waiting for it.

4 · Generation Loss — Telefone (~25 min)
  • Telephone, except the machine is every player. Under your two columns, band 3 · Generation Loss — play Telefone: press Send this page to Telefone →. That opens the Telefone tab (after Notebook) with your shaped page as Pass 0 · yours. No AI connected yet? Change model opens ⚙ Settings → AI — Groq is the one to pick.
  • Ask it to — three requests, all ticked: clean it up & correct it · fix grammar, spelling, punctuation · rewrite in clear, standard English. Press AI Revise: the machine gets your page and those requests, and what comes back pops up as Pass 1. Press it again — pass two is made from pass one, not from yours. Then ×10 and watch the tabs fill (it stops at 20).
  • Read the strip. Under each pass is how much of your page is still there. Where does it stop dropping? That flat line is the machine's idea of a finished page — Chiang's blurry JPEG, except the picture is yours and you can watch the detail go. Under the writing, switch on Show what this pass changed and go back to Pass 1: red is what it cut, green is what it put in. What went first?
  • Watch it: fragments become sentences, wisdoom becomes wisdom, the labyrinth untangles, the double voice merges into one — and the line breaks close up.
  • Save round — it keeps Pass 0 beside the last pass, with the model, the count and the date, in the Rounds list below. Then Start over, untick two of the requests or Change model, and play the same page again. Two or three rounds.
  • Reflect in the Generation Loss reflection box under your rounds (it's the same box back on One-Pager 5, under band 3): What did the machine fix first? What is gone by the end that was there at the start? What refused to go? What does that mean for your writing — and for the writing of your students?

Purpose: feel, don't just hear, the machine's pull to the mean — and locate exactly what only you make. This is the hinge into the currere.

5 · The Look-Back (~20 min)
  • Reread all five one-pagers — Why You Write, Writing Place, Voice Print, Show Don't Tell, Breaking the Rules. The Look-Back: what keeps showing up? Across the place, the voices, the moment, the rules you break — name the thread that recurs.
  • Write one short entry in your Writer's Notebook naming that thread. It's a candidate for your currere — the longer self-study you begin in Act II. And Whitman's question from this morning, now that the machine has had its turn: what's the verse it couldn't take?

Purpose: five weeks of small pieces were laying down material — today you find the line running through them, and Act I hands off to Act II.

Wrap-up & at home:
  • Finish Breaking the Rules (keep it broken) and the reflection. If the page changed much after class, play one more round on the finished version, so a saved round matches the page you submit. Then at the bottom of One-Pager 5 press Export One-Pager (PDF). One PDF: your One-Pager, your writing session and AI-use log, your Generation Loss reflection, and your saved Telefone rounds. That's the whole submission. (Due Fri · Canvas.)
  • Bring your currere thread to Week 6, and read Moore, "Starting the Conversation" (the currere how-to). It's where your currere starts.
