    Fix one — the five tools

    Rewrite whichever of C, D, or F the room ranked telliest. Same moment, same people, none of its summary words. Use at least three of these.

    • Create mind pictures. Find the sentence that summarizes ("we waited for what felt like forever") and explode it into a scene: who moved, what they touched, what was on the TV nobody watched.
    • Add sensory detail. One sense the passage hasn't used yet. What did the room smell like? What did the chair do under you?
    • Precise words. Not the car — her mom's silver CR-V. Not the food — name the dish. (And notice: F has precise nouns and still tells. Precision helps only when it's doing the feeling's work.)
    • Pump up the verbs. Did she go into the room, or shuffle, limp, stride? Mary Oliver didn't write that a man's eyes "turned into ice" — she wrote they brittled into it.
    • Weed the garden. Cut the adverb that repeats the verb ("swaggered arrogantly"), and every big word that names the feeling: warm, amazing, nervous, depressing, relief, excited, free.

    Tools adapted from Penny Kittle, Tom Romano, Judy Rowe Michaels, and Maja Wilson, "Tinkering/Revising for Fearless Writing." Their list has eight; the other three — sound and rhythm, sentence length, placement and payoff — get their own day.
