Week 4 · Wednesday — Build the moment

Act I · Make Me Taste It · what does your moment look, sound, and taste like?
Today's goals: Sharpen one sensory rep, then compose Show, Don't Tell — cut every word that tells instead of shows. Due Friday.

0 · Daily Poem — "Mid-Term Break" (~10 min)

  • Seamus Heaney, "Mid-Term Break" [Canvas]. Read it aloud. Heaney never says he's grieving — he shows it: the knelling bells, the baby cooing, the old men standing to shake his hand, and the last line. Where does the grief land, and how does he get it there without naming it?

Purpose: the standing ritual — and the day's move at its highest level: an emotion shown, never told.

1 · Housekeeping (~5 min)

  • Questions on the Show, Don't Tell one-pager. Everyone bring their gush?

2 · "Make me taste it" — one revision rep (ch13) (~15 min)

  • From your gush, find one line that tells ("it was scary," "the food was good"). Rewrite it so we feel it — the way Romano turns "melon and ham" into a description that makes your mouth water.
  • Read the before/after to a neighbor. Did the rewrite put them in the moment?

Purpose: practice the exact cut-and-rebuild move on your own words before you build the whole page.

3 · Compose Show, Don't Tell in Journaler (~30 min)

  • Build your Show, Don't Tell one-pager from your gush — one small moment, one page, all senses. Cut every word that tells instead of shows.

Purpose: get most of the page down in the room, so the take-home is polish, not a blank start.

4 · Peer read (~15 min)

  • Trade with a neighbor. Mark the one line that most put you in the moment, and one place you still want to be shown instead of told.

Purpose: a reader tells you fast where the showing is working and where it isn't.
