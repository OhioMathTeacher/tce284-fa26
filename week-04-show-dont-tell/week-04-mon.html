Week 4 · Monday — Show, Don't Tell

Act I · Make Me Taste It · can you put us inside a moment through the senses?
Today's goals: See how vivid description beats "melon and ham," then launch Show, Don't Tell and gush one small moment rendered through the senses.

0 · Daily Poem — "Blackberry Eating" (~10 min)

  • Galway Kinnell, "Blackberry Eating". Read it aloud. Which words make you taste it — "the black art of blackberry-making," the lumpy words he loves to "squeeze, squinch open, and splurge"? Point to the exact ones.

Purpose: the standing ritual — and a poem that does today's move (words you can taste), so the target is in the room before we name it.

1 · Housekeeping + Voice Print debrief (~5 min)

  • One line of dialog from your Voice Print that sounded exactly like the person.

2 · Reading discussion — Romano + J. Edwards (~20 min)

  • WWM ch12, "The Sense of It" (pp. 53–55), in pairs: Romano writes a boyhood game in pure sensation ("Lungs seared from frigid air… Danny's gloves… burned and stank"). Mark two lines that put you inside it. What sense is each hitting?
  • J. Edwards, "Beyond Presence" — the Invisibility scene only, with a new partner: how does she make you feel the moment from the inside? Mark two sensory details. Notice she's going back into a real moment and rebuilding it through the senses — the move you'll make.
  • Quick whole-group share.

Purpose: work both readings through a task — name the difference between telling and showing before you have to do it.

3 · Sensory warm-up — make me taste it (~20 min)

  • Romano's ch12 exercise (~12 min): pick a remembered activity — cooking, playing, working, a sport. Take 2 minutes to jot the senses you remember. Then write straight into it for 10 minutes — all senses, no explaining.
  • Trade and compare (~8 min): Romano's example — a flat menu says "melon and ham," but the vivid version makes your mouth water. Trade your paragraph with a neighbor: which line makes them taste it? Where did you tell instead of show? A machine gives you "melon and ham"; the specific, sensory, true detail is yours.

Purpose: two fast reps at showing over telling — Romano's own, on a small target, before the real one.

4 · Start the one-pager + gush your moment (~25 min)

  • Read the assignment (~5 min): open the Show, Don't Tell handout together — one small moment from your life, rendered through the senses, one page. Look at what it asks and how it's graded.
  • Gush it in Journaler (~5 min): start the 5-minute timer and gush the moment — the light, the sounds, the smells, what your hands felt, what was on your tongue. Don't tell us how it felt; make us feel it. Gush mode locks editing.
  • After the buzzer (~10 min): answer the app's few questions about how it went, then pick the one moment you'll build into your page. Keep this draft — you compose the page Wednesday.

Purpose: get the sensory raw material down in the room, before the editor — and leave with a moment chosen.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
  • Bring your gush Wednesday — we compose the page.
  • Come back to your gush and add three sensory details you left out.
