Week 2 · Monday — Your Writing Place

Act I · Writing Place · where do you write, and how do you know yourself as a writer?
Today's goals: Read Wiederhold and Romano on the blank page and the writing place, read a gallery of writers' places like a photographer, then launch OP2 — Writing Place and gush your first draft. Tonight you photograph where you work.

0 · Daily Poem — "Where I'm From" (~10 min)

  • George Ella Lyon, "Where I'm From". Read it aloud. What do you notice — the lists, the specific names, the foods and sayings?
  • The poem is built to be borrowed. Where are you from? Jot a few of your own lines — the objects, smells, and sayings only your place had.

Purpose: the standing ritual — and a place poem you can emulate, opening the Writing Place theme with your own roots.

1 · Housekeeping + OP1 debrief (~10 min)

  • Journaler working for everyone? Questions from week 1.
  • Quick OP1 debrief — one line from your Gush you didn't expect to write. How did trusting the gush go?

2 · Reading discussion — Wiederhold (~20 min)

  • Wiederhold, "How to Read a Blank Page of Writing" (Currere Exchange Journal), in pairs: (a) a time a blank page froze you — what was going on? (b) Wiederhold says people freeze for different reasons — name one he gives. (c) as a future teacher, how would you meet a student staring at a blank page?
  • Our returning lens (whole group): Wiederhold steps back and examines the writing self instead of just telling a story — why we freeze, how we come to know ourselves at the page. Where do you see that stepping-back? It's a move you'll make in your own writing.
  • This week's chapter is WWM ch5, "A Writing Place" (you read it tonight). Carry Romano's question — what does your writing place have to have? — straight into the photo-read next, and into OP2.

Purpose: work the currere through a task, add the lens we bring to every model writer, and point ch5's question at today's making.

3 · The photo-read — look at writers' places (~20 min)

  • Look together at photographs of writers' rooms, cabins, and offices — Pollan's hut, Gaiman's gazebo, Dahl's shed, Didion at work, Will Self's wall of maps. (Romano runs this with Jill Krementz's The Writer's Desk.)
  • Read the images the way Romano does: What's in the frame? What isn't? What's the writer doing? What angle and distance was it shot from? Notice how many show the empty room — your OP2 needs one shot of you at work. How would you frame that?
  • Sketch the two or three shots you'll take tonight — choose your angles before you pick up the camera.

Purpose: you see your own place more sharply after reading someone else's — and you shoot with intention, not on autopilot.

4 · Launch OP2 + gush your writing place (~15 min)

  • Meet OP2 — Writing Place: Romano's own one-pager (your place + process + 2–3 photos, one of you at work). Look at the examples.
  • In Journaler, 5-minute timer, gush: where and how you write — light, sound, tools, talismans, rituals, when the writing comes. Gush mode locks editing.

Purpose: get the words down before the photos, so the page is built on real observation, not a caption.

Wrap-up & tonight:
  • Photograph your writing place, and have someone photograph you working in it. Shoot the angles you sketched — a close-up, a low angle, fill the frame with what matters. Bring them Wednesday.
  • Come back to your gush and add three sensory details you left out.
  • Read WWM ch5–8 (pp. 23–39, in Canvas Files) for Wednesday.
