Week 10 · Monday — How a Currere Lands

Act II · currere · mentors · the synthetical close · revision studio
Today's goals: See how a currere weaves its thread and lands, write your own synthetical close, then use the studio time to revise from your conference and peer review.

0 · Daily Poem — "The Road Not Taken" (~10 min)

  • Robert Frost, "The Road Not Taken". Read it aloud. Notice the speaker isn't in the woods — he's looking back, "ages and ages hence," making a story out of a choice: "I shall be telling this with a sigh." How does meaning get made only in the looking-back?

Purpose: the synthetical move in miniature — a life-thread understood only in hindsight, which is exactly the close you write today.

1 · How a currere closes — skim & discuss (~20 min)

  • You read WWM ch19, "Mentors, Imitation." Romano's move: find a writer you admire and borrow how they do a thing. Today you borrow an ending.
  • Skim just the endings — the last page or two — of a few curreres, and find one whose close you'd want to borrow:
    • Edwards, "Who's Cheating Whom?" (our first one)
    • Daspit, "None of Us"
    • Romano, "The Anchor, the Bridge, the Lesson"
    • Bird, "Self-Coaching Emotions" (see its "Full Circle Narrative" close)
  • Use the Week 1 lens: where does the writer go back into a real moment, and where do they step back to say what it means now? Then together: what did these endings do? What makes a close land instead of just stop?

Purpose: study the close as a craftable thing — the weave and the landing — across a few real curreres, then name what makes an ending land before you write your own.

2 · Studio — write your close, then revise (~45 min)

  • Quiet writing time. Start with the hardest part: your synthetical close. It's not a section you tack on — it's the weave and the landing, where your past, present, and future moments finally speak to each other and you say what the thread means now. Borrow the ending you marked. Don't summarize — make new meaning, the way Frost's speaker does looking back.
  • Then keep going through the whole draft, acting on what you already have: your conference notes and the peer-review moves — a sharper lead, an earned metaphor, the scenes you rendered.
  • I'll circulate. Grab me for the one thing you're stuck on.

Purpose: one long protected block to do the real writing — the close and the revision are the same work, and most of it happens now if the room is quiet and I'm in reach.

Wrap-up & before Wednesday:
  • Keep revising toward your Final Draft (due Fri Oct 30). Bring a version you'd be willing to read a piece of aloud.
  • Wednesday we record the audio for your author page and go hunting for the genres buried in your currere — the start of Act III.
