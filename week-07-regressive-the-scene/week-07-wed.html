Week 7 · Wednesday — Storyboard Your Scenes

Act II · currere · two past · two present · two future
Today's goals: Turn your thread into six scenes that reach across time, then write your way into each one.

0 · Daily Poem — "Those Winter Sundays" (~10 min)

  • Robert Hayden, "Those Winter Sundays". Read it aloud. A small remembered scene, and the meaning arrives only at the end — "What did I know, what did I know of love's austere and lonely offices?" How does one scene carry a whole relationship?

Purpose: a single rendered scene, understood in hindsight — exactly what your storyboard cards are reaching for.

1 · Drawing it out (WWM ch18) (~10 min)

  • You read ch18, "Drawing It Out." With a partner: what does Romano say drawing does that outlining doesn't? Why might a picture find a scene your sentences would miss?

Purpose: give them permission to draw before they write — the picture surfaces the moment.

2 · Storyboard — six scenes across time (~30 min)

  • Take six index cards. On each, draw a single scene from your thread — a moment, not a summary. Rough sketches; no one's grading the art.
  • Spread them across time: two past, two present, two future. Two you lived as a learner or young teacher, two from your life now, two you can imagine ahead.
  • Lay the six out and re-sequence them. Which order makes the thread feel true? The timeline order is rarely the strongest one.

Purpose: force the thread across all three times before drafting, so the future and present don't get lost behind one vivid past.

3 · Write around the card (~25 min)

  • Tape your six cards onto a large sheet of paper. For each card, set a timer and write around it for a few minutes — what happened, what it looked and sounded like, what you notice now.
  • Let it spill into the margins. As Romano puts it, writing is expansive, literally. Don't polish. Get the scene down.

Purpose: turn each drawn moment into raw prose while the picture is in front of you — the seed of every scene in the draft.

Wrap-up & over the weekend:
  • Turn your six cards into six rough scene drafts — a paragraph or two each, still messy. Bring them Monday. This is the material your First Draft is built from.
