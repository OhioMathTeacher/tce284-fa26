Week 9 · Monday — Read It Aloud: Leads & Metaphor

Act II · currere · revise for the lead and the image
Today's goals: Read your first draft aloud and revise it through two lenses from your reading — a lead that pulls the reader in, and a metaphor that carries the thread — then work them in threes.

0 · Daily Poem — "Metaphors" (~10 min)

  • Sylvia Plath, "Metaphors". Read it aloud. The whole poem is a riddle built only of images — "an elephant, a ponderous house," "a melon strolling on two tendrils" — and she never says the plain word. What does saying it in metaphor do that the plain word couldn't?

Purpose: a poem that is its metaphors — the exact move you're hunting for in your own draft today.

1 · Metaphor & leads (WWM ch16–17) (~15 min)

  • You read ch16, "Metaphor" and ch17, "Leading the Way." With a partner, name the two lenses for today:
  • The lead — what does Romano say an opening has to do? What's the difference between a lead that starts and one that clears its throat first?
  • Metaphor — how does one image carry meaning a plain sentence can't? Think "crystal stair" — a whole hard life in one figure.

Purpose: turn the reading into two concrete things to look for, not a discussion — the lenses you'll revise through in the next hour.

2 · Read-aloud self-revision (~20 min)

  • Read your own first draft aloud, quietly, to yourself. Your ear catches what your eye skips.
  • Mark two things as you go: (1) where does the lead warm up before it really begins — could you cut into a later, stronger opening line? (2) where does a moment plead for an image — a place a metaphor could carry the thread?

Purpose: hear your own draft first — most of the lead-and-image fixes, you'll find yourself, out loud.

3 · Peer review in threes (~30 min)

  • In groups of three, take turns: each writer reads a piece of the draft aloud while the other two listen (draft in hand).
  • The group works only the two lenses: the lead — does the opening pull us in, or warm up first? Where would you start it? · metaphor — where could one image carry the thread? Offer the writer a candidate.
  • Each writer leaves the circle with two concrete moves — a sharper lead and one earned metaphor to try.

Purpose: the reader hears the draft land on real listeners, and the focus stays on craft you can act on — not "I liked it."

Wrap-up & before Wednesday's conference:
